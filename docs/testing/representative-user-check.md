# Representative teacher check

Set up one session with a teacher who follows up on classroom observations. Give them a working account in a practice environment and ask them to use two fictional student names. Use a low-stakes example from their subject that they can revisit with a paper exercise or with two simulated responses. Ask them to think aloud while you observe and give help only when they ask to continue.

1. Create a class and add the two fictional students. After a lesson example, write one short note in your own words about what both students did. Save it, leave the screen, and return to the note.
2. Open the suggested changes. Keep a suggestion that reflects what happened, edit one to make it more accurate, and decline one you would leave out. Apply your choices and tell the observer what you expected to happen.
3. Open one student's page and find the observation and the original note. Open the class's list of issues, choose the shared issue, and check who is affected and how each student's experience differs.
4. Record one thing you tried with the two students together. Schedule a short check for both and open it from the **Due** list when it appears. Check that you can find what you tried before.
5. Imagine that one student completes the paper check independently and the other needs help. Write a single outcome note for both students and review the suggested observations. For this exercise, mark the first student's issue resolved for now. Decide whether the second student's issue should stay open or move to improving. Return to the student and class pages and find the new observations and their current status.
6. Enter a later note in which the first student struggles with the issue again. Decide whether to reopen it. Find the new observation and the earlier resolution in the student's history.

## Observer notes

Record what the teacher did, where they paused, and when they asked for help. Use anonymous session notes.

- Ask whether recording the first note felt lighter than entering separate fields or making separate records. Record the teacher's comparison and any work they repeated.
- Note the steps and friction when the teacher kept, edited, and declined suggestions. Ask whether they understood what would be applied.
- Record what the teacher learned or did in the student page and in the class issue page. Ask when they would use each one.
- Record whether seeing the earlier observation, practice, or resolution changed a later teaching decision. Capture the specific decision and the history they used.
- Record whether opening a due check led to a new saved observation for each student. Ask the teacher to find both observations and the outcome note that supports them.

Leave the record blank until a teacher completes the session. Use what happened in the session to identify places where the workflow helped and where it added work.
